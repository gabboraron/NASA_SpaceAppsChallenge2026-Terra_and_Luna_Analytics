## What the challenge asks for (from 01-hackathon-map.md, §4.2)
A tool that (1) helps pick Earth-science variables, (2) gets them for a region, (3) builds and visualises a time series long enough for statistics, and (4) estimates the rate of change and tests significance (t-test, Mann-Kendall, confidence intervals). "A response that does both the visualization and the analysis meets the full scope." The audience is high-school and college students. The brief explicitly suggests: a short question flow ("what/where would you like to investigate"), simple variable definitions, AI that suggests variables likely to show trends for a region type, and removing the seasonal cycle. It also says a non-significant trend is still interesting.
→ Idea 1 maps almost one-to-one onto the objectives. Idea 2 maps onto the "Approach" considerations, but on its own it would not do the statistics that make up the core scope.

## Statistical method (this is where "Validity" points are won)
- **Pre-processing:** monthly data → (a) deseasonalise with anomalies against the monthly climatology, or (b) the **Seasonal Kendall** test (`pymannkendall.seasonal_test`), or (c) annual / water-year means. Area-mean over the region with **cos(latitude) weights**. Require at least ~20 years and flag shorter records (SMAP).
- **Trend test:** Mann-Kendall plus **Sen's slope**. Use pymannkendall 1.4.3 (Jan 2023; https://pypi.org/project/pymannkendall/), which provides `original_test`, `hamed_rao_modification_test`, `yue_wang_modification_test`, `pre_whitening_modification_test`, `trend_free_pre_whitening_modification_test`, `seasonal_test`, `regional_test`, `sens_slope`. It returns `trend, h, p, z, Tau, s, var_s, slope, intercept` but **no slope confidence interval**, so use `scipy.stats.theilslopes` for the 95 % CI. Also show OLS + t-test as the "textbook" comparison the brief names.
- **Autocorrelation pitfall:** serial correlation inflates MK significance. Default to **Hamed–Rao modified MK** (or TFPW, Yue–Wang 2002) and show the raw-MK vs. corrected p-value side by side. This teaches the point and protects Validity.
- **Multiple testing on maps:** testing every pixel at α=0.05 produces about 5 % false "significant" pixels. Apply the **Benjamini–Hochberg FDR** (`scipy.stats.false_discovery_control`) and stipple only pixels that survive. Wilks (2016) "The Stippling Shows Statistically Significant Grid Points", BAMS 97:2263, doi:10.1175/BAMS-D-15-00267.1, recommends FDR and notes that FDR is robust to spatial correlation. We recall, without having checked the full text, that he suggests α_FDR = 2·α_global (0.10) for strongly correlated fields. Caveat from EGU26 (Schutte et al.): FDR controls a global error rate, so individual stippled pixels are not "locally significant". Say so in the UI.
- **Other honesty features (cheap to build):** sensitivity to start/end year (for Peru, the 1997–98 and 2015–16 El Niños can create or remove a trend), so add a "change the period" slider. Version and sensor breaks (IMERG V07 ends 2025-09, the GRACE→GRACE-FO gap). "Significant ≠ large ≠ caused by climate change." Plain-language note when a trend is not significant: "might need a longer record" (the brief's own point).
- **Reference implementation** for the per-pixel case: the Google Earth Engine non-parametric trend tutorial (https://developers.google.com/earth-engine/tutorials/community/nonparametric-trends). It assumes "suitably large and uncorrelated" samples; we go one step further.

## Prior art and competition (relevant to Creativity)
- **Giovanni** (NASA GES DISC): menus of variables plus area-averaged time series, maps and downloads. **No significance testing** and no guidance on which variable to choose. Our tool starts from a question and adds statistics on top of the same kind of data. https://giovanni.gsfc.nasa.gov/
- **KNMI Climate Explorer**: has trend + significance tools and downloads, but is expert-oriented. Its own help page lists limits ("cannot define your own indices, nor upload data… or handle large datasets"). https://climexp.knmi.nl/help.cgi
- **Climate Engine** (DRI / U Idaho / Google, built on GEE): on-demand anomaly maps and time series with 70+ datasets. Aimed at land managers. We did not confirm that it offers MK/FDR. https://www.drought.gov/data-maps-tools/climate-engine
- **Trends.Earth** (Conservation International, QGIS plugin): NDVI trend and land-degradation analysis. https://github.com/ConservationInternational/trends.earth
- **GEE non-parametric trend tutorial** (MK + Sen per pixel), but no autocorrelation or multiple-testing handling. https://developers.google.com/earth-engine/tutorials/community/nonparametric-trends
- **NASA–Microsoft Earth Copilot** (Nov 2024, Azure OpenAI, plugs into VEDA; at announcement it was tester-only, not public). https://blogs.microsoft.com/blog/2024/11/14/from-questions-to-discoveries-nasas-new-earth-copilot-brings-microsoft-ai-capabilities-to-democratize-access-to-complex-data/ . The GitHub repo `microsoft/Earth-Copilot` now resolves to **microsoft/Planetary-Explorer** (MIT, "not a supported Microsoft product"). It has STAC search, raster sampling, GEOINT modules, and an "Extreme Weather agent" doing **linear-regression trends on CMIP6 projections**, deployed on Azure AI Foundry. **This is the direct competitor for Idea 2.** Our differentiator is **observational records + autocorrelation-corrected MK + FDR + uncertainty teaching + reproducible export**, not "chat with EO data".
- **NASA/PO.DAAC CMR MCP server** (https://github.com/podaac/cmr-mcp), Element 84 "Queryable Earth" agentic EO, and an agentic dataset-search preprint (arXiv 2601.14176): LLM dataset discovery is becoming standard. Do not pitch it as the novelty.
- **NASA–IBM Prithvi** foundation models: not needed. They do not help significance testing, so leave them out.
- **Past Space Apps:** 2024 global winners included Connected Earth Museum (AI-guided climate gallery with temperature time series) and TerraTales (regional climate stories) (https://www.nasa.gov/learning-resources/stem-engagement-at-nasa/nasa-international-space-apps-challenge-announces-2024-global-winners/). In 2020 the "Planet at Your Fingertips" data-exploration challenge asked for time series and anomaly detection. We found **no past winner that combines trend significance with an educational guide**, which is an open niche (moderate confidence: 2025 winners not inspected).

## Risks (and mitigations)
1. **Data-download time on Saturday** (Earthdata auth, file counts, regridding). Mitigation: start the earthaccess pre-fetch by 11:00. POWER plus GRACE single-file is the guaranteed fallback. Keep grids coarse (0.25–0.5°).
2. **Dead or slow services:** Data Rods now redirects to a discontinuation notice. AppEEARS can sit queued for days. The Giovanni API is point-only, needs a token and has a quota. IMERG Final ends 2025-09 (V08 pending). Nothing live in the demo path except POWER and our own cache.
3. **Validity traps:** autocorrelation, multiple testing, short records (SMAP, MODIS ≈25 yr is fine), El Niño endpoint effects, GRACE gap, mixing reanalysis (POWER/MERRA-2) with observations. Mitigation: label each source type ("satellite obs" / "model / reanalysis"). Benedek reviews every verdict text.
4. **LLM failure modes:** wrong variable, invented numbers, overclaiming causation. Mitigation: catalogue-only function calling, numbers injected from JSON, a fixed disclaimer, and a no-LLM wizard fallback. Deploy the Azure OpenAI model in the first hour; if quota or region is blocked, use any OpenAI-compatible key.
5. **Scope creep / integration on Sunday:** feature freeze at 12:00 Sunday. Video recorded by 14:00.
6. **Hosting:** GoDaddy free hosting is likely PHP/static shared hosting (not verified), so use it only for the domain. Have Streamlit Community Cloud as a backup host.
7. **Creativity ceiling:** judges may see "another Giovanni". Lead the pitch with the question-first, teach-the-uncertainty angle and with the local Hungarian case.

## Recommendation
Build the **combined** version: Idea 1 as the core, with Idea 2 as a thin, constrained front-end. Drop Idea 2 as a standalone product. On its own it scores about 14/25: it duplicates Earth Copilot / Planetary Explorer and skips the significance testing that defines this challenge's scope. The combined tool scores an estimated 23–24/25. It covers every objective plus the brief's "AI suggests variables" and "short series of questions" hints, and it can be done in ~65–70 person-hours against ~72 available, *provided* that:
- (a) regions and variables are curated (3 × 5) and pre-fetched Saturday;
- (b) the LLM only routes and explains, and never computes; and
- (c) a no-LLM wizard path exists.

Pitch angle: "Most tools show you a line. We tell you whether it is real." Show the autocorrelation-corrected MK, the FDR-stippled map and the honest "not significant yet" verdict. Use southern Peru (Yhair's home region) plus Hungary's Homokhátság for local judges.
